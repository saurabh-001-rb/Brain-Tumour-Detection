Glioma Tumor

Intra-Axial

Gliomas arise from supportive glial cells (astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, ependymal cells). They represent the most common primary malignant brain tumors in adults.

MRI Hallmarks: Infiltrative, ill-defined margins, perifocal vasogenic edema, heterogeneous contrast enhancement with central necrosis in high-grade lesions (GBM).

Meningioma Tumor

Extra-Axial

Originates from arachnoid cap cells of the meningeal coverings. Typically slow-growing and benign (WHO Grade I), though mass effect can cause significant cranial neuropathy.

MRI Hallmarks: Well-circumscribed, broad-based dural attachment, intense homogeneous gadolinium enhancement, and pathognomonic 'dural tail' sign.

Pituitary Adenoma

Sellar / Endocrine

Arises within the anterior lobe of the pituitary gland in the sella turcica. Causes endocrine hypersecretion or optic chiasm compression leading to visual field deficits.

MRI Hallmarks: Sellar enlargement, suprasellar extension with 'snowman' or 'figure-eight' appearance, displacement of normal pituitary bright spot on T1.

Normal Brain (No Tumor)

Parenchymal

Symmetrical cerebral and cerebellar hemispheres, normal ventricular size without displacement, preserved gray-white differentiation, and absence of pathologic mass effect.

MRI Hallmarks: Intact midline structures, clear cortical sulci, symmetrical lateral ventricles, absence of abnormal focal contrast uptake.
